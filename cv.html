Quant AI Market Lab | Independent quantitative research | completed v1.0.0 | 2026 | Repository

  • Built a validated eight-ETF workflow covering returns, covariance, PCA, KMeans and Gaussian HMM market-condition models, Ridge and Random Forest forecasts of next-five-day SPY return RMS, and long-only minimum-variance portfolios.
  • Verified return identities, covariance symmetry/PSD properties, eigendecomposition and explained variance. The first three PCs explained 79.01% of standardized historical return variation; on 565 common chronological dates Ridge MAE was 0.003201 versus 0.003443 for Random Forest.
  • Compared five covariance methods, three transaction-cost assumptions, nine benchmark scenarios and 32 strategy-period robustness checks. Minimum-variance approaches reduced realized volatility in reported subperiods but underperformed SPY buy-and-hold on raw return; May-August 2026 are described as pseudo-out-of-sample rather than an untouched holdout.
  • Compared 2-6 Gaussian HMM states, diagonal/full covariance options, multiple initializations, likelihood, AIC/BIC, occupancy and chronological diagnostics; retained an interpretable three-state full-covariance specification without claiming economically proven regimes. Examined Ridge coefficient stability across 87 monthly model refits.

Quant Market Lab II - Signal Processing & Prospective Forecasting | v0.1 baseline frozen; extensions in progress | 2026 | Repository

  • Fixed the information cutoff at 31 August 2026; implemented Ridge, constant and rolling 20-day RMS baselines at four lead times, preserved 12 versioned September-December forecasts, and recorded model/data/source fingerprints with target-timing and prefix-invariance tests.
  • Across 87 historical zero-gap windows Ridge had the lowest average absolute error in the repository comparison. September remains a retrospective check; nine October-December five-session forecasts were published from the frozen August-cutoff CSV without refitting. I implemented EWMA with separately frozen August/October information sets, then reproduced signed, squared and absolute-return autocorrelation without changing the forecasts. November and December outcomes remain pending; autoregressive extensions remain future work.
  • Evaluated the first completed October five-session window: realized SPY daily-return RMS was 0.5263%, compared with the frozen rolling-20 forecast of 0.5780%, Ridge forecast of 0.8118% and constant baseline of 0.8791%. This is one evaluation window, not proof of predictive superiority.
  • Reproduced August-data lag-one autocorrelations of -0.116 (signed), 0.420 (squared) and 0.352 (absolute returns). Verified the preserved original adjusted-price CSV against its SHA-256 manifest and documented differences between later download vintages. Kept reconstructed EWMA experiments separate from the original frozen forecasts.

Payment Guard | Completed synthetic fraud-review ranking study | 2026 | Repository · Evidence and limits

  • Audited 1,090,000 synthetic AMLNet v2 transactions, reconciled 1,411 positive labels, inspected nested metadata and potential target leakage, prepared chronological splits, verified a SQLite import and investigated training-period patterns using SQL.
  • Implemented regularized logistic regression and XGBoost with pandas/NumPy, scikit-learn preprocessing, chronological training and model selection, sigmoid/isotonic calibration, feature ablation and paired review-budget analysis. Frozen March–April 2026 final holdout: 253,190 transactions, 278 fraud labels; at the precommitted top-1% review capacity (2,532 reviews/model), XGBoost captured 267 positives versus logistic regression's 254 (+13 captured labels). Logistic captured 225 versus 222 at the smaller 0.1% budget; superiority was conditional on the chosen objective and workload.
  • Analyzed feature sensitivity: XGBoost at 1% captured 169 positives without category and 141 without both category and type (267 with all features). Decision-time provenance of these synthetic columns remains unverified. Published aggregate-only plots and metrics, freeze and hash checks, and an auditable evaluation; 186 tests passed locally. This is a completed synthetic-data research milestone, not a deployed bank fraud-detection system or established financial savings.

MATHEMATICAL & COMPUTATIONAL RESEARCH

Approximating Mixed Volumes to Arbitrary Accuracy | 2025 | Hariharan Narayanan and Sourav Roy | arXiv paper

  • Co-authored a randomized approximation algorithm for mixed volumes of convex polytopes with integer vertices, combining convex optimization, Lorentzian polynomials and polytope subdivision; established multiplicative-error and failure-probability guarantees with polynomial runtime for a fixed number of distinct input polytopes under the stated assumptions.

Master's Thesis: Approximation of Mixed Volumes of Special Convex Bodies | TIFR Mumbai | March 2026 | Supervisor: Prof. Hariharan Narayanan | Thesis PDF

  • Studied mixed volumes of a polytope and Euclidean ball using Minkowski sums, the Steiner polynomial, geometric decomposition, sampling and log-concavity; developed and analysed noisy binary search for a scaling parameter, including decision-error and runtime bounds, while distinguishing the idealized oracle model from practical approximate optimization.
  • Implemented random-walk samplers for estimating volumes of convex bodies, including ball walk and hit-and-run variants, together with geometric membership/decomposition procedures and mixed-volume computations.

Exploring the Efficient Computation of Permanents of Graph Laplacians | TIFR | December 2022 | Supervisor: Prof. Piyush Srivastava | Project report PDF

  • Proved the permanent analogue of Brenner's result for diagonally dominant matrices; explored tensor-product formulations, recursive expressions for paths, cycles and trees, and Pfaffian methods.

RSA Encryption and Decryption in Python | INSPIRE Summer Project, Presidency University Kolkata | 2019; revised 2026 | Repository

  • Implemented educational RSA key generation and encryption/decryption using Miller-Rabin primality testing, the extended Euclidean algorithm and modular exponentiation. Mixed Volume Approximations | Python and C++ numerical experiments | October 2026 | Repository

  • I implemented numerical versions of the framework in my master's thesis, Approximation of Mixed Volumes of Special Convex Bodies (Chapters 4–6): B1 estimates volume, B2 samples a geometric component label, and B3 estimates component probabilities. Algorithms 1–3 organize sampling, scaling-parameter search and mixed-volume estimation. I checked the cube-plus-ball estimates against exact formulas and ran matched Python/C++ pilot comparisons. Finite burn-in, correlated samples and floating-point calculations do not establish the ideal-oracle guarantees or certify mixing and accuracy.
  • Compared matched Python/C++ pilot calculations across dimensions 7, 9 and 11 and scale parameters 0.5, 1.5 and 3.0; measured execution time and numerical error against exact cube cases, with reproducibility settings and limitations recorded in the repository.

Experiments on Random Walks | Computational convex geometry | Python/C++17 | October 2026 | Repository · Interactive research website

  • Implemented Ball Walk, Dikin Walk, Coordinate Hit-and-Run, Random Hit-and-Run and a cube-specialized John's Walk on high-dimensional cubes, with readable explicit and optimized Python/C++17 code. Published source-labelled convergence checkpoints and cost comparisons for dimensions 1–10, with separate dimension-20 experiments. Distinguished empirical marginal/pairwise diagnostics from rigorous total-variation mixing; a browser JavaScript implementation allows exploratory runs but is not a native-language benchmark.

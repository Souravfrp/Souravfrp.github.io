COMPLETED HISTORICAL STUDY · v1.0.0

Quant AI Market Lab

Can I measure market risk and compare forecasting and portfolio decisions honestly?

I studied eight ETFs using validated returns, PCA, KMeans, Gaussian HMMs, Ridge, Random Forest and long-only minimum-variance portfolios. Chronological comparisons and explicit trading timing keep the experiments tied to the information available at each decision.

Ridge: 0.3201 percentage-point MAERandom Forest: 0.3443 on the same 565 historical forecasts of five-session SPY log-return RMS.

What I learned: Minimum-variance portfolios had lower realized volatility in the reported subperiods, while equal-weight and SPY finished with higher wealth in the zero-cost full-period comparison. Lower risk did not mean higher return.

Scope: Retrospective evidence, with documented costs, numerical checks and model limits. It does not establish a profitable live strategy or identify the best future ETF.

Quant Market Lab II

What happens when I preserve a forecast and wait for its target window?

I preserved 12 September–December baseline forecasts using a 31 August cutoff. The October 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 window is now complete: I compared the frozen predictions with observed returns and reconciled the displayed prices with full-precision Yahoo data.

Observed October daily RMS: 0.5263%Rolling-20: 0.5780% · Ridge: 0.8118% · Constant: 0.8791%. All three overpredicted; rolling-20 was closest in this window.

COMPLETED SYNTHETIC ML RESEARCH · MERGED 21 OCTOBER 2026

Payment Guard

With a fixed review capacity, which payments should a model prioritize?

I validated 1,090,000 synthetic AMLNet v2 transactions and their chronology, explored the SQLite database using SQL, then compared regularized logistic regression and XGBoost using deliberately separated development periods. The final March–April evaluation used a precommitted 1% review-budget protocol and previously fitted models.

Frozen 1% holdout: XGBoost 267 vs logistic 254 positives caught253,190 synthetic transactions · 278 positive labels · 2,532 reviews per model. XGBoost caught 13 additional positives (96.04% vs 91.37% recall), not 13 percentage points or 13% higher accuracy.

What I implemented: pandas/NumPy preprocessing, scikit-learn pipelines, XGBoost, imbalanced-classification diagnostics, sigmoid/isotonic calibration, fixed review-budget evaluation, category/type ablations, paired model disagreement, Matplotlib visualizations, SHA-256 artifact verification and 186 locally passing Python tests.

Why the category result changes the interpretation

At 1% capacity, full-feature XGBoost captured 267 positives, but its separately fitted no-category variant captured 169 and its no-category-and-type variant 141. The synthetic categorical fields have not been verified as available at real payment-decision time. The full review queues of XGBoost and logistic shared only 1,210 of 2,532 transactions.

What I cannot conclude: These are retrospective results from synthetic data, not demonstrated real-bank fraud performance, statistically established superiority, saved financial losses or a deployed review/block policy. The holdout was chronologically reserved for final scoring but earlier dataset audits had inspected it. The code and aggregate results are public; fitted model files and row-level predictions remain local.

RULE-BASED BASELINE PUBLISHED · 10 OCTOBER 2026

ResearchAudit

What evidence supports a research claim, and what still needs checking?

I froze Quant AI Market Lab v1.0.0 and built a Python rule-based checker that screens source patterns, records file coverage and produces reviewable reports. I also tested its isolated transaction-cost function.

34 Python files screened · 52 test methods passed10 candidate findings were triaged. Twenty designed examples run within one test; these are not independent detection-accuracy measurements.

What I found: Claim 7 is supported for the tested cases, with two input-validation gaps: near-unit weight sums can overallocate, and two-dimensional weights can trigger broadcasting. I preserved the audited source and added tests that record these behaviours.

The other six claims retain their provisional source-inspection labels. I confirmed no look-ahead error at the flagged locations; that does not certify the entire project as leakage-free.

Limits and next step: I have not reproduced the full backtest numerical results or implemented the proposed multi-agent system. Variable-valued settings, aliased split imports and some seed paths remain checker blind spots. I need independent labelled cases and a second frozen target before claiming broader audit reliability.

PYTHON AND C++ EXPERIMENTS · FROZEN OCTOBER CHECKPOINT

Mixed Volume Approximations

These labels come from my master's thesis, Approximation of Mixed Volumes of Special Convex Bodies (TIFR, March 2026, Chapters 4–6). B1 estimates the volume of the polytope plus a scaled ball; B2 returns a geometric component label from a sampled point; B3 estimates the component probabilities through repeated sampling. Algorithm 1 describes B2 sampling, Algorithm 2 searches for a useful scaling parameter, and Algorithm 3 combines the volume and component probability to estimate a mixed-volume coefficient.

I implement numerical versions of the oracle framework from my master's thesis for a centered cube plus a scaled Euclidean ball. I compare volume and mixed-volume estimates with exact cube formulas, then compare Python and C++ calculation time under matched settings.

My theoretical framework uses stated ideal-oracle assumptions. These finite-walk, floating-point experiments do not establish certified mixing or accuracy guarantees and do not assess the validity of my theorem or proof. A faster implementation does not remove sampling uncertainty.
